The Carson Discovery Method

A Disciplined Sequence, Applied to Every Book.

Six stages, followed in order, every time. Strategy only enters after research, never before.

01
Stage One

Research

Understand the author, books, catalog, audience, platforms and existing visibility before anything else is discussed.

Focus

The author's background, publishing history, platform and prior marketing activity.

Output

A clear, evidence-based picture of the current situation, not assumptions.

02
Stage Two

Diagnose

Identify where discovery is working, where it may be breaking down, and what deserves closer attention.

Focus

Gaps between effort and visibility, mismatches between positioning and audience.

Output

A prioritized list of what's actually limiting discovery right now.

03
Stage Three

Discover

Find overlooked opportunities across readers, platforms, communities, positioning and the wider market.

Focus

Comparable books, reader communities, underused platforms, untapped positioning angles.

Output

A set of specific, researched opportunities worth evaluating.

04
Stage Four

Strategize

Turn research into a practical strategy based on the author's actual situation, not a generic playbook.

Focus

Which opportunities are worth pursuing, in what order, with what resources.

Output

A specific, prioritized plan tailored to the book and author.

05
Stage Five

Execute

Put the chosen opportunities into action through the appropriate marketing channels.

Focus

Implementation across the channels identified as worth pursuing.

Output

Visible, reader-facing activity aligned with the strategy.

06
Stage Six

Measure

Review what happens, learn from the response, and refine the strategy accordingly.

Focus

What actually moved the needle, and what didn't.

Output

Refined priorities, feeding back into research for the next cycle.

A Note on the Method

This Is a Cycle, Not a Checklist.

Measurement feeds back into research. As a book, its catalog, or its market changes, the method repeats, keeping strategy aligned with evidence rather than habit.
