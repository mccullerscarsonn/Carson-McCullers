About Carson

Good Marketing Starts With Understanding.

Carson McCullers is an independent book discovery and marketing strategist with 8 years of experience helping published authors understand why their books are, or aren't, being found.

Most conversations about book marketing start in the wrong place. An author arrives with a problem, low sales, flat visibility, a stalled launch, and is handed a list of tactics before anyone has actually looked at the book, the author's platform, or the market it sits in. Carson works differently. Before recommending anything, she researches the situation in front of her: the author, the book, the catalog, the audience, and the current state of visibility across the platforms that matter.

Over 8 years of working independently with authors across genres and publishing paths, one pattern has held consistently: the authors who feel like marketing "isn't working" are rarely lacking effort. They're lacking a clear, evidence-based understanding of where their book is actually visible, where it is invisible, and why.

How I Think About Book Marketing

A Few Things I Believe, Stated Plainly.

01

I don't believe every author needs more marketing.

I believe authors need a clearer understanding of what is preventing discovery. More activity without more clarity usually just produces more noise.

02

Generic marketing language tends to hide the real problem.

"Grow your audience" and "boost visibility" sound productive, but they don't tell you where the gap actually is. Precision matters more than enthusiasm.

03

Every author has a different discovery problem.

A debut literary novel, a twelve-book romance catalog, and a traditionally published memoir are not solved with the same playbook, even if the surface symptoms look similar.

04

Research should happen before outreach, not after.

I look closely at an author's situation before I ever propose a direction, because recommendations made without research are just guesses dressed up as strategy.

"I'm not interested in telling authors what every book needs. I'm interested in finding out what a specific book is missing, and whether that gap is actually worth closing."

Working independently means every project is handled personally. There is no handoff to a junior team member, no templated strategy pulled from a shared folder. Authors who work with Carson are working directly with the person doing the research, forming the strategy, and staying with the project through execution and measurement.
