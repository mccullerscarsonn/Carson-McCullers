The Real Problem

Being Published Is Not the Same as Being Discovered.

Publishing a book places it in the marketplace. It does not automatically place it in front of the readers most likely to care about it.

Visibility Is Not Even

A book can be live on every platform and still be functionally invisible to the readers who would respond to it most.

Positioning Shapes Discovery

How a book is categorized, described and compared determines whether the right readers ever encounter it in the first place.

Effort Isn't Evidence

Marketing activity and marketing results are not the same thing. What's been tried matters less than what it actually produced.

Carson's Philosophy

Research First. Strategy Second.

Before any recommendation, Carson maps the full picture, the author, the book, the catalog, the audience, the market and the current state of discovery.

The Carson Discovery Method

A Six-Stage Approach, Not a One-Time Fix.

Every engagement follows the same disciplined sequence, because strategy that skips research is just a guess with better formatting.

01 — Research

Understand the author, books, catalog, audience, platforms and existing visibility.

02 — Diagnose

Identify where discovery is working, where it may be breaking down, and what deserves closer attention.

03 — Discover

Find overlooked opportunities across readers, platforms, communities, positioning and the wider market.

04 — Strategize

Turn research into a practical strategy based on the author's actual situation.

05 — Execute

Put the chosen opportunities into action through the appropriate marketing channels.

06 — Measure

Review what happens, learn from the response, and refine the strategy.

On Pricing

Marketing Problems Rarely Come in Standard Sizes.

An author with one recently published book has a different situation from an author with twelve titles. A traditionally published historical novel may require a different strategy from an independently published nonfiction book.

Because of this, recommendations and investment are determined after understanding the specific situation, not before.
